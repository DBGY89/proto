What does running locally actually mean?

Cloud vs. local, what localhost is, and when each option makes sense. Explained without assuming you are an engineer.

Running something locally means your computer does all the work, not a server somewhere else on the internet.

Once you understand this, you will never be confused by the difference between something working on your machine and something working for everyone else. It sounds technical. It turns out to be obvious.

the cloud vs. local

Think of it like food. The cloud is a restaurant: someone else has the kitchen, you place an order, you get the result. Running locally is cooking at home: you own the kitchen, nothing leaves your house.

The cloud
Someone else's kitchen.

You send a request to a server. They process it, send back the result. You pay per use. ChatGPT, Google, Notion — all cloud. Fast and powerful, but your data goes through their pipes.

Local
Your kitchen.

Everything runs on your machine. No request leaves. No one else sees your data. No subscription, no usage limits, no internet needed after setup.

what localhost means

Localhost is just the name your computer uses to talk to itself. The number after the colon, like 3000 or 8080, is the port — think of it as the door number. When you see localhost:3000 in your browser it means: open the app running on my machine, through door 3000. Nothing is on the internet. Only you can see it.

Your task this week SOP 04
Run your landing page locally and compare it to the live version.

Open index.html directly in your browser. Notice the URL says file:// instead of http://. That is local. Now open your GitHub Pages URL. Look at both side by side. If something looks different, that is your first debugging exercise.

When done A real understanding of the difference between local and live that comes from experience, not from reading about it.
