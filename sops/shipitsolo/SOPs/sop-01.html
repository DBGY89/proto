Block 00 — Before you start 01

How to write a PRD with AI

A PRD is not a document for engineers. It is a thinking tool. Use it before you open any other tool in this bundle.

A PRD is a single page that answers the questions you should answer before building anything: what is this, who is it for, what does it do, and what does it not do.

Ten minutes of clarity here saves you hours of frustration later. Every time. Without exception.

Most people skip this and open the AI coding assistant with a vague idea. Two hours later they have something that is almost right but does not hold together. The PRD exists to prevent that.

the seven fields
  • What it does
    One sentence. If you cannot say it in one sentence, you have not defined it yet. Do not move on until this is clear.
  • Who it is for
    Be specific. Not "anyone who wants to" but "a designer who works solo and needs to test ideas fast."
  • The problem it solves
    What is the situation before this exists? What friction does it remove?
  • What it includes in v1
    List only what is in the first version. This list should be shorter than you think.
  • What it does not include
    Every feature you explicitly exclude is a decision not to get distracted. Write it down.
  • The tech stack
    What tools will you use? At this stage: an AI coding assistant, GitHub, and GitHub Pages. If unsure, leave blank and ask your AI assistant.
  • Edge cases
    What happens when something goes wrong? Empty form submissions. No data to show. API failures.

how to fill it in
1
Paste the paragraph you wrote in SOP 00
Open your AI assistant. Paste your landing page description. That is your starting point.
2
Paste the template and ask for help
Copy the template below and add it to the conversation. Then say: "Help me fill this in based on what I just described. Push back if anything is vague."
3
Do not skip "what it does not include"
For your landing page: no blog, no CMS, no animations unless they serve a purpose. Every item keeps you focused.
4
Save the final PRD
A notes app, a document, anywhere. You will paste it into your AI coding assistant in SOP 03.
what your PRD might look like
Example PRD — Personal landing page
What it does:A personal page that introduces who I am, what I do, and lets people contact me. Who it is for:Potential clients or collaborators who want to know more after a first contact. The problem it solves:Right now when someone searches for me they find nothing or something outdated. What it includes in v1:My name and what I do. A short bio. Selected work or skills. A contact form. Social links. What it does not include:A blog. A CMS. Animations. Multiple languages. A dark mode toggle. Tech stack:AI coding assistant, GitHub Pages for hosting, Supabase for saving contact form messages. Edge cases:Form submitted empty: show a validation error. Submission fails: show a retry message.
the template

This is one way to structure a PRD. Not the only way. As you build more you will notice which fields you actually use and what you wish you had written down. For now, use this one.

PRD Template
WHAT IT DOES: [One sentence. What does this product do?] WHO IT IS FOR: [Be specific. Describe the person, not a market segment.] THE PROBLEM IT SOLVES: [What is the situation before this exists?] WHAT IT INCLUDES IN V1: [List only what ships on day one. Keep it short.] WHAT IT DOES NOT INCLUDE: [Explicitly list what you are not building.] TECH STACK: [What tools will you use? If unsure, ask your AI assistant.] EDGE CASES: [What happens when something goes wrong?]
